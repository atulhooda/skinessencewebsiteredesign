## Step 1: Soothe and Cool Treated Skin During the First 48 Hours

The first 24 to 48 hours after a clinical session are the most critical. Whether you had a [chemical peel](/blog/chemical-peel-aftercare), [acne scar treatment](/blog/adult-acne-treatment-in-pune), or laser session, treated skin often looks pink, feels warm, or shows minor swelling ([Standard guidelines of care for chemical peels](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-chemical-peels/), [Laser hair removal: FAQs](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-faqs)). Proper cooling steps help reduce burning sensations, pain, swelling, and crusting right after laser procedures ([Complications of laser and light-based devices therapy in patients…](https://ijdvl.com/complications-of-laser-and-light-based-devices-therapy-in-patients-with-skin-of-color/)).

### Immediate Cooling Measures

Cold compresses or calamine lotion can be applied to soothe sensitive skin ([Standard guidelines of care for chemical peels](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-chemical-peels/)). When heat feels intense after laser work, ice packs help relieve burning sensations ([Standard guidelines of care: Lasers for tattoos and pigmented lesions](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-lasers-for-tattoos-and-pigmented-lesions/)). Active cooling before and after laser pulses also prevents excess heat buildup in tissue ([Standard guidelines of care: Lasers for tattoos and pigmented lesions](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-lasers-for-tattoos-and-pigmented-lesions/)). Always wrap ice packs in a clean cloth. Placing bare ice straight onto fragile skin can cause cold burns or irritation.

### Gentle Hydration and Barrier Support

Keep your home skincare very simple during the initial two days. Dermatologists advise using broad-spectrum sunscreens and only bland moisturisers until peeling is complete ([Standard guidelines of care for chemical peels](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-chemical-peels/)). Avoid scented creams, essential oils, or thick body lotions on treated facial skin. Gentle hydration keeps the skin barrier soft and flexible while it repairs itself.

### The Hands-Off Rule

As the skin heals, it may feel dry, tight, or slightly itchy. You must strictly avoid scratching or peeling the skin ([Standard guidelines of care for chemical peels](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-chemical-peels/)). Rubbing or scratching skin treated with a peel can cause an infection ([Chemical peels: FAQs](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/younger-looking/chemical-peels-faqs)). Patients with skin of colour can safely get chemical peels, but they should [visit a dermatologist skilled in darker skin tones](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/younger-looking/chemical-peels-faqs) to avoid long-lasting pigment problems ([Chemical peels: FAQs](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/younger-looking/chemical-peels-faqs)).

## Step 3: Pause Gym Workouts, Heat Exposure, and Makeup

Many fitness enthusiasts in Kalyani Nagar and Viman Nagar enjoy daily workouts. However, strenuous exercise requires a short pause after aesthetic procedures. Pausing intense heat and sweat allows your skin barrier to reset without added irritation.

### Postpone Heavy Exercise and Heat

High-intensity gym sessions, hot yoga, and running raise your core body temperature and cause heavy sweating. Sweat carries salts that can sting tender skin. Increased blood flow to the face also worsens swelling and redness. Pause steam rooms, saunas, and hot showers for a few days to avoid heat buildup. Gentle indoor walking is a much safer option during this window.

### Hold Off on Heavy Cosmetics

You might feel tempted to cover pink skin with foundation before a meeting or a dinner in Koregaon Park. However, thick makeup can clog open pores and delay healing. Makeup sponges and brushes can also carry bacteria to delicate skin. Let your skin breathe without foundation or concealer for at least 24 to 48 hours.

### Pause Strong Active Ingredients

During recovery, stick only to bland moisturisers until all peeling finishes ([Standard guidelines of care for chemical peels](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-chemical-peels/)). Dermatologists often prime Indian skin for 2 to 4 weeks before a procedure using retinoids, glycolic acid, or depigmenting creams to cut complication risks ([Complications of laser and light-based devices therapy in patients…](https://ijdvl.com/complications-of-laser-and-light-based-devices-therapy-in-patients-with-skin-of-color/), [Standard guidelines of care for chemical peels](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-chemical-peels/), [Standard guidelines of care for acne surgery](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-for-acne-surgery/)). Once your procedure is done, stop these strong home actives. Wait until your dermatologist checks your skin and confirms it has healed completely.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is strict sun protection vital after aesthetic procedures for Indian skin?

Indian skin typically falls between Fitzpatrick types IV and VI, containing higher amounts of epidermal melanin in larger melanosomes. This makes it more prone to persistent post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation after lasers or peels if exposed to sunlight.

### How should I soothe treated skin during the first 48 hours?

Apply cloth-wrapped ice packs, cold compresses, or calamine lotion to reduce burning sensations, pain, and swelling. Use only broad-spectrum sunscreen and bland moisturisers while avoiding scented creams or active ingredients.

### What should I do if my skin begins to peel or feel itchy?

Strictly avoid rubbing, scratching, or peeling flaking skin, as doing so can cause infections, scarring, and stubborn pigmentation. Keep the skin supple with bland moisturiser and let shedding happen naturally.

### When should I contact my dermatologist about post-procedure symptoms?

Reach out to your dermatologist promptly if your treated skin burns, itches intensely, or develops abnormal swelling, rather than attempting unverified home remedies.
