## Understanding the Hair Cycle and What Triggers Diffuse Shedding

To understand telogen effluvium, you must look at how hair grows. Each scalp hair follows an independent cycle. The anagen phase is the active growth stage. The catagen phase is a brief transition. Finally, the telogen phase is a resting stage. A person normally sheds an average of 100 hairs each day under baseline conditions ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).

A sudden shock can disrupt this normal rhythm. When this happens, a large cohort of growing hairs enters the resting phase prematurely ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). In people with telogen effluvium, about 30% of scalp hairs stop growing and shift into the telogen resting phase ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). Because of this shift, daily shedding can surge to an average of 300 hairs per day ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).

There is a notable delay between the shock and the visible hair fall. Resting hairs remain anchored in the scalp for two to four months before they shed ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). For this reason, acute hair loss frequently becomes apparent roughly three months after the initial precipitating event ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). Common triggers identified in clinical settings include:

- Episodes involving high fever and severe systemic infections ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss), [Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).
- Major surgical procedures or acute physical trauma ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss), [Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).
- Rapid weight loss, sudden dietary starvation, or acute malnutrition ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss), [Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).
- Periods of severe psychological or emotional stress ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss), [Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).
- Abrupt hormonal transitions, such as after childbirth or during menopause ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).

In approximately one-third of people with telogen effluvium, no specific trigger can be identified ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). In these cases, a clinical evaluation is essential. A dermatologist can assess your overall health, rule out underlying disorders, and provide clear guidance.

## Dermatological Evaluation: Differentiating Effluvium from Pattern Alopecia

A key step during consultation is differentiating diffuse shedding from pattern hair loss. Telogen effluvium is a reactive disturbance. In contrast, pattern hair loss (androgenetic alopecia) is a progressive genetic condition. The two issues require very different clinical management. Scalp evaluation often includes [trichoscopy](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss), although its role in telogen effluvium is limited ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)).

Under trichoscopy, telogen effluvium shows non-specific features across the entire scalp ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). These features include yellow dots that represent empty hair follicles and upright regrowing hairs ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). There is also an absence of features characteristic of other scalp diseases ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). In scalp hair, a proportion of greater than 20% telogen hairs is considered diagnostic of telogen effluvium ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)).

In contrast, androgenetic alopecia exhibits distinct diagnostic signs on trichoscopy ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)):

- Hair shaft thickness diversity (anisotrichosis) of 20% or greater ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)).
- A reduced number of triple hairs and an increased number of single hairs per follicular unit in the mid-frontal scalp compared to the occipital area ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)).

An accurate dermatological diagnosis provides clarity. It prevents patients from spending resources on unproven hair-fall oils and shampoos. It also allows the doctor to design an appropriate recovery strategy.

## Investigating Systemic Triggers Through Clinical Blood Profiles

Most acute shedding episodes settle once the body recovers. However, when diffuse hair shedding persists for more than six months, it is classified as chronic telogen effluvium ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). Chronic shedding occurs due to chronic primary telogen effluvium or secondary medical disorders ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)).

Doctors may use blood tests to check for conditions such as thyroid abnormalities that may be contributing to hair loss ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). Underlying medical disorders linked to chronic shedding include iron deficiency anaemia, thyroid disease, and malnutrition ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss), [Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). Checking these internal markers helps identify issues that require medical management.

Hormonal imbalances can also influence hair changes in women. [Polycystic ovary syndrome](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome) (PCOS) is a common example. PCOS affects an estimated 10% to 13% of women of reproductive age worldwide, though up to 70% of affected women remain undiagnosed ([Polycystic ovary syndrome](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome)). The condition involves inappropriate hormonal signalling that leads to higher than normal androgen levels ([Polycystic ovary syndrome](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome)).

High androgen levels can cause oily skin, persistent acne, excess facial or bodily hair, and hair loss from the head ([Polycystic ovary syndrome](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome)). PCOS is also a chronic metabolic condition that increases long-term risks for insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and obesity ([Polycystic ovary syndrome](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/polycystic-ovary-syndrome)). Identifying these hormonal patterns ensures that clinical care addresses the broader internal picture.

## Expected Timelines for Shedding Cessation and Visible Recovery

Understanding the recovery timeline helps establish clear expectations during diffuse shedding. Telogen effluvium is typically a self-limiting condition that resolves over time ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). New hairs begin growing immediately after resting hairs shed, though noticeable density takes several months to return ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).

Typically, hair loss begins two to four months after the event that triggered the problem and lasts approximately six months ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). Most cases run their course within six to nine months ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)). New hairs begin growing immediately after the hair falls out, but significant growth may not be noticed for several months ([Telogen effluvium](https://www.health.harvard.edu/a_to_z/telogen-effluvium-a-to-z)).

Hair grows at a modest pace of about one centimetre each month. Because of this, visible fullness takes time to become apparent. Trichoscopy may show upright regrowing hairs across the scalp as new strands emerge ([Trichoscopy of generalised noncicatricial hair loss](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/trichoscopy-of-generalised-noncicatricial-hair-loss)). Routine clinical follow-up visits allow the doctor to observe shedding levels and verify that hair growth is progressing as expected.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is telogen effluvium?

Telogen effluvium is a reactive shift in the hair cycle where physical stress, illness, or hormonal changes push around 30% of growing hairs prematurely into the resting phase. This leads to diffuse hair shedding across the scalp, which often becomes noticeable two to four months after the triggering event.

### How long does telogen effluvium shedding usually last?

Hair shedding typically starts two to four months after the trigger and lasts about six months, with most cases resolving within six to nine months. Because new hair grows approximately one centimetre each month, visible density takes several months to return.

### How do dermatologists differentiate telogen effluvium from pattern hair loss?

A dermatologist evaluates the scalp using clinical examination and trichoscopy. Telogen effluvium shows generalized empty follicles and upright regrowing hairs, whereas pattern hair loss displays hair shaft thickness diversity of 20% or greater and altered hair counts across different scalp regions.

### Can hormonal conditions like PCOS contribute to diffuse hair loss?

Yes, polycystic ovary syndrome involves elevated androgen levels that can cause hair loss from the head along with oily skin, persistent acne, and excess facial or body hair.
