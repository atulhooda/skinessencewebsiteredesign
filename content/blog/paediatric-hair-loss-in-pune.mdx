## Common Medical Causes of Paediatric Hair Loss in Pune

Hair loss in adults often involves genetics, hormonal shifts, or progressive miniaturisation of follicles. In contrast, paediatric hair loss is usually an acute biological or mechanical reaction. Children do not experience patterned genetic balding. Instead, their shedding usually points to fungal infections, physical strain on hair roots, or localised autoimmune reactions.

The most common infectious cause in school-age children is [tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis), a fungal infection of the scalp skin and hair shafts. Tinea capitis characteristically presents with partial hair loss accompanied by localised inflammation, erythema, and scaling ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)). The fungus invades the hair follicle and weakens the structural protein of the shaft. Because of this, hairs snap easily under minor tension.

Hair loss from this condition can present in different ways across the scalp ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)):

- Single patches of hair loss that are often accompanied by scaling ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).
- Multiple patches of hair loss across the scalp ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).
- Involvement of individual hairs or small hair clusters ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).

Physical traction is another frequent cause in school-age children. Tight school braids, tightly pulled ponytails, or taut patka headwear place chronic tension on hair roots along the hairline and temples. In other cases, smooth circular patches of sudden hair loss may indicate alopecia areata. This is an autoimmune response where the body's immune system temporarily halts follicular growth without causing scaling.

## Differentiating Fungal Infections from Cradle Cap and Dry Flakes

Parents often find it difficult to tell harmless flakes apart from an active fungal infection. During infancy, many babies develop [cradle cap](/blog/cradle-cap-treatment-in-pune), also called [infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap). It classically presents as a non-inflammatory breakout of greasy, yellowish scales that affect the vertex and frontal areas of the scalp ([Cradle cap (infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap)). Cradle cap is usually not itchy, and infants are otherwise generally well ([Cradle cap (infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap)).

The development of cradle cap may be related to hyperactivity of sebaceous glands responsive to residual maternal androgens, and Malassezia yeast has also been implicated ([Cradle cap (infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap)). While cradle cap is common in infants, lingering scaling or unusual flaking in older children needs careful evaluation.

Tinea capitis is an important scalp condition that can resemble cradle cap, but it presents with distinct clinical features ([Cradle cap (infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap)):

- Hair shaft involvement: Cradle cap sits on the skin surface without snapping the hair. In fungal ringworm, hairs may be broken either above or at the scalp level ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).
- The 'black dot' appearance: Swollen broken hair stubs broken at the level of the scalp characterise the [black dot ringworm pattern](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp) ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).
- Degree of inflammation: Cradle cap classically presents as a non-inflammatory eruption of greasy scales ([Cradle cap (infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap)). In contrast, tinea capitis characteristically presents with partial hair loss accompanied by some degree of inflammation, itch, or erythema ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)).
- Diffuse scaling variants: In some children, a non-inflammatory diffuse scale variant of tinea capitis resembles generalised dandruff, where hair loss is subtle or absent ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)). This makes clinical examination essential to avoid treating an infection as plain dryness.

## Why Vigorous Oiling and DIY Remedies Can Worsen Scalp Inflammation

In many Indian households, applying warm coconut, almond, or mustard oil and massaging the scalp is a familiar tradition. When a child begins shedding hair or develops flaky patches, reaching for hair oil is often a parent's first reflex. However, when the scalp has an active fungal infection or inflammatory condition, heavy oils can do more harm than good.

Heavy oils create a thick layer over the scalp skin. This barrier traps dead skin cells, sweat, and environmental dust. Furthermore, Malassezia has already been implicated in scalp scaling and cradle cap ([Cradle cap (infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/cradle-cap)). Adding heavy oils to an irritated scalp can trap debris and make the scaling worse.

Rubbing and massaging also create mechanical risk for weakened hair roots. When fungal infection weakens the hair shaft, friction from hand pressure or stiff combs causes hair to snap easily. This mechanical strain accelerates hair breakage and can widen thin patches. On inflamed scalp skin, forceful rubbing can also introduce bacteria into irritated follicle openings, causing tenderness and redness.

Children with sensitive skin may also react poorly to unverified home remedies, such as onion juice, camphor, or garlic pastes. These ingredients can cause contact dermatitis, burning sensations, and skin irritation. An inflamed scalp requires gentle barrier protection and medical guidance, rather than kitchen remedies.

## What to Expect During a Gentle Dermatological Scalp Examination

Parents often worry that a clinical visit will frighten their child or involve painful tests. Modern dermatological scalp assessments are gentle, collaborative, and non-invasive. They are designed to keep young children relaxed throughout the appointment.

A dermatologist uses non-invasive visual tools to evaluate hair loss and scalp health:

- Scalp dermoscopy (trichoscopy): Scalp dermoscopy is a fast and non-invasive procedure useful for confirming tinea capitis, showing predictive features such as comma hairs, corkscrew hairs, zigzag hairs, and Morse code-like hairs ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)). Dermoscopy can also reveal scale and broken hairs visible as black dots ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).
- Wood lamp examination: This non-invasive screening step uses ultraviolet light in a darkened room. Bright green fluorescence is observed in hairs affected by Microsporum species ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)). Nonfluorescent endothrix Trichophyton infections do not show this glow, except T. schoenleinii, which displays a dull grey-green glow ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)).
- Potassium hydroxide (KOH) microscopy: A light microscope examination of wet-mounted scalp scrapings or plucked hairs rapidly confirms tinea capitis by showing fungal hyphae and spores ([Tinea capitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/tinea-capitis)).
- Gentle sampling methods: While standard testing uses scrapings and plucked hair follicles ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)), younger children may not tolerate plucking. In these cases, sterile brushes such as disposable toothbrushes or adhesive tape stripping provide practical alternative sampling methods ([Tinea capitis (scalp)](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/tinea-capitis-scalp)).

## Frequently asked questions

### What are common causes of sudden hair shedding in children?

Common causes include fungal infections such as tinea capitis, physical traction from tight hairstyles, and autoimmune conditions like alopecia areata.

### How can you tell cradle cap apart from a fungal scalp infection?

Cradle cap typically presents as non-inflammatory, greasy yellow scales on infants without broken hair shafts. In contrast, fungal infections often involve inflammation, scaling, and hairs snapped at or above the scalp level.

### Why should heavy hair oils be avoided on an irritated child's scalp?

Heavy oils can trap dead skin cells, sweat, and debris, which may worsen scaling and yeast growth. Vigorous rubbing also causes weakened, infected hair shafts to snap more easily.

### What happens during a clinical examination for paediatric hair loss?

Dermatologists use non-invasive diagnostic tools including scalp dermoscopy to view follicle patterns, Wood lamp examinations to check for fluorescence, and gentle hair or scale sampling.
