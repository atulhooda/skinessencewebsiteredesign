## Understanding Your Lesion: Benign Moles, Viral Warts, and Skin Tags

Before undergoing any removal procedure, it is essential to recognise that skin growths differ fundamentally in their biological origin and anatomical depth. A technique suitable for a narrow, superficial skin tag can prove entirely ineffective or counterproductive when applied to a viral wart or a deep dermal mole. A comprehensive clinical consultation allows a dermatologist to categorise the lesion accurately:

- Benign Dermal Moles (Naevi): Clusters of melanocytic cells that may present as flat or raised spots. Electrosection is well suited for excising benign dermal naevi ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). Attempting amateur removal often only cuts off the top of the mole, leaving portions of the lesion down in deeper layers of the skin, failing cosmetically and complicating future proper removal ([DIY Don'ts: Why At-Home Mole Removal Is a Bad Idea](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/diy-donts-why-at-home-mole-removal-is-a-bad-idea/)).
- Skin Tags (Acrochordons): Soft, small outgrowths that tend to develop in friction-prone areas such as the neck, underarms, and groin folds. In clinical electrosurgery, these lesions can be excised with electrosection or treated using electrofulguration ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)).
- Viral Warts (Verrucae): Protruding epidermal lesions that can be treated with modalities such as electrofulguration, during which thermolysis generates smoke and fumes that can contain Human Papillomavirus (HPV) particles ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). They commonly arise on the fingers, palms, elbows, and soles of the feet.
- Seborrhoeic Keratoses and Dermatosis Papulosa Nigra (DPN): Protruding warty lesions frequently observed on the face, neck, and upper body that dermatologists can treat or shave using electrofulguration and electrosection ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)).

Understanding the distinct nature of each lesion is essential. For viral warts, electrosurgical destruction requires careful clinical technique because the resulting fumes can carry HPV particles ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). Attempting to pick at, abrade, or remove warts and moles at home also carries significant hazards. When an individual attempts DIY removal, there is no way to determine whether a lesion is benign or malignant, creating a dangerous risk of incompletely excising a melanoma whose cells can remain in the skin and spread ([DIY Don'ts: Why At-Home Mole Removal Is a Bad Idea](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/diy-donts-why-at-home-mole-removal-is-a-bad-idea/)).

When a growth is treated without clinical assessment, partial removal remains a frequent complication. If residual portions of a mole remain down in deeper layers of the skin, the cosmetic outcome fails, and the resulting tissue damage complicates future proper removal ([DIY Don'ts: Why At-Home Mole Removal Is a Bad Idea](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/diy-donts-why-at-home-mole-removal-is-a-bad-idea/)).

## Clinical Removal Techniques: Radiofrequency Ablation and Precision Excision

Modern clinical dermatology relies on advanced electrosurgical modalities to ensure lesions are treated safely and with minimal tissue trauma. Electrosurgical systems use high-frequency alternating electric currents to generate controlled thermal energy for cutting and coagulation ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)).

In electrosurgery, high-frequency alternating electric current at 200 to 10,000 V is passed through the skin to generate heat, with radiofrequency devices operating at frequencies exceeding 1,500 kHz for fine cutting ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). This approach allows electrosection to combine precise tissue cutting with vessel coagulation while causing minimal peripheral heat damage ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)).

### Radiofrequency Electrosection

Electrosection is suited for excising relatively vascular lesions, including protruding dermal naevi, skin tags, and seborrhoeic keratoses ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). By combining precise surgical cutting with rapid microvascular coagulation, this technique produces minimal peripheral heat damage in surrounding tissue ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). Consequently, patients benefit from reduced post-procedure pain, less swelling, a lower risk of infection, and enhanced wound healing that yields subtle, discreet cosmetic outcomes ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). Furthermore, because collateral thermal destruction is tightly constrained, excised tissue margins remain well preserved should histological evaluation be indicated ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)).

### Electrofulguration and Environmental Safety

For small, protruding, or superficial lesions such as delicate skin tags, viral warts, and dermatosis papulosa nigra, dermatologists frequently utilise electrofulguration ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). In this technique, the active electrode is held 1 to 2 mm away from the skin surface, generating a controlled electric spark that dehydrates and destroys the target tissue without requiring mechanical incision ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)).

Procedural safety during wart destruction also demands strict attention to surgical plumes. The electrosurgical destruction and thermolysis of viral warts generate smoke and fumes that can contain HPV particles, bacteria, carcinogens, and toxic gases such as benzene and formaldehyde, posing an exposure hazard if inhaled ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). In a clinical environment, medical teams manage these procedural fumes with specialised extraction and ventilation protocols to maintain high standards of patient and operator safety.

## Scar Minimisation and Pigmentation Control for Indian Skin Types

Conducting minor skin procedures on Indian skin tones requires a careful clinical understanding of ethnic skin physiology. In pigmented skin types, melanocytes respond actively to inflammatory events, making medical expertise essential for managing post-procedure recovery and protecting against unwanted colour changes.

In [darker skin types](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ethnic-dermatology), melanocyte size, shape, and production rates are greater than in lighter skin, and melanin is contained in larger melanosomes ([Ethnic dermatology](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ethnic-dermatology)). The enzyme tyrosinase, which catalyses the production of melanin, is also more active in darker skin ([Ethnic dermatology](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ethnic-dermatology)). In addition, melanosomes in darker skin tend to degrade at a slower rate than those in fair skin ([Ethnic dermatology](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ethnic-dermatology)).

Cutaneous inflammation results in the release of inflammatory mediators that stimulate melanogenesis by melanocytes, leading to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in pigmented skin ([Ethnic dermatology](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ethnic-dermatology)). When skin trauma occurs, this increased melanin production can leave noticeable dark discolouration at the affected site.

Clinical precision is therefore critical. Modalities like radiofrequency electrosection minimise peripheral heat and collateral tissue damage during lesion removal ([Electrosurgery](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/electrosurgery)). By limiting surrounding tissue trauma, dermatologists help minimise unnecessary cutaneous inflammation, reducing the triggers that lead to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation on Indian skin.

This biological insight also helps dermatologists harmonise lesion removal with other cosmetic programmes. For patients in Pune undergoing laser hair reduction, acne treatment, or [acne scar treatment](/concerns/acne-scars), sequencing is planned with medical care. For instance, removing dark, raised moles or vascular skin tags before starting [laser hair removal across the face or body](/blog/laser-hair-removal-indian-skin) ensures that hair reduction lasers do not inadvertently deliver concentrated heat into pre-existing pigmented lesions.

## Post-Procedure Recovery: Daily Care and Wound Healing Guidelines

Achieving an unblemished cosmetic outcome depends as much on diligent aftercare as it does on procedural precision. Dermatological aftercare focuses on supporting natural tissue regeneration, preventing infection, and shielding healing skin from external irritants.

- Maintain Cleanliness and Hygiene: Proper postoperative care and wound cleanliness are vital, as poor sanitation and post-procedure neglect significantly increase the risk of infection and delayed healing ([DIY Don'ts: Why At-Home Mole Removal Is a Bad Idea](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/diy-donts-why-at-home-mole-removal-is-a-bad-idea/)). Follow your dermatologist's exact guidance on keeping the site clean and dry.
- Allow Scabs to Detach Naturally: A fine, protective crust forms over the treated area within a few days. It is essential never to scratch or forcefully peel this crust away, as physical disruption of healing tissue can result in chicken pox-like indented scars or bumpy hypertrophic marks ([DIY Don'ts: Why At-Home Mole Removal Is a Bad Idea](https://www.skincancer.org/blog/diy-donts-why-at-home-mole-removal-is-a-bad-idea/)).
- Rigorous Sun Protection: Because inflamed skin in darker skin types readily releases mediators that stimulate melanocytes and induce hyperpigmentation ([Ethnic dermatology](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/ethnic-dermatology)), protecting vulnerable tissue from direct sunlight is crucial. Shield the healing area from Pune's sun using physical cover, clothing, and broad-spectrum sunscreen once permitted by your doctor.
- Pause Active Skincare: Temporarily hold strong cosmetic actives, chemical exfoliants, retinoids, and fragranced formulations away from the treated area until complete tissue recovery is confirmed by your doctor.

Downtime following radiofrequency ablation is typically brief. Superficial crusting on the face generally resolves over several days as the skin regenerates, whereas lesions treated on the trunk or limbs may take somewhat longer to re-epithelialise. Most patients comfortably return to their routine professional and personal schedules immediately following their outpatient visit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is radiofrequency ablation preferred over salon snipping for mole removal?

Radiofrequency electrosection cuts and coagulates tissue with minimal peripheral heat and collateral damage, reducing pain, infection risk, and scarring. Salon snipping or at-home attempts lack sterile protocols and depth control, creating a high risk of infection, delayed healing, and indented or hypertrophic scars.

### How does clinical removal prevent post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation on Indian skin?

Indian skin contains more active tyrosinase and larger melanosomes that produce excess pigment when exposed to inflammation. Dermatologists use precision technologies like radiofrequency to limit collateral tissue trauma and thermal damage, significantly reducing the inflammatory triggers that cause dark spots.

### What happens during the post-procedure healing period?

A fine crust forms over the site within a few days and must be allowed to detach naturally to avoid scarring. Daily recovery requires keeping the wound clean, using rigorous sun protection to avoid pigmentation, and avoiding harsh cosmetic actives until healing is complete.
