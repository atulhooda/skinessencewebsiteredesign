## Recognising Pale Patches: Symptoms and Stages

Pityriasis alba is a low-grade dermatitis that mainly affects children and adolescents aged 3 to 16 years ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). The name describes what clinicians see on the skin. The word 'pityriasis' refers to fine scales, while 'alba' refers to the pale colour of the patches.

The patches usually appear on the face, neck, and shoulders ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). These light patches are typically asymptomatic, meaning they do not usually itch or hurt ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). Children rarely complain of pain or irritation from them.

Dermatologists note that pityriasis alba typically evolves through a few clear stages ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)):

- Early stage: A patch starts as a slightly scaly pink patch or plaque with a just palpable papular surface ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)).
- Scaling hypopigmented stage: The mark turns into a pale patch or plaque with fine surface scale ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)).
- Macular stage: The patch then becomes a post-inflammatory light macule without scale ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)).
- Resolution: The skin colour gradually returns completely to normal over time ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)).

Because these stages take time to unfold, parents may feel that simple moisturising is not working. Understanding this gradual timeline helps families remain patient while the surface barrier repairs itself.

## Why Light Spots Appear: Barrier Health and Sun Exposure

To care for a child's skin, parents should understand why these light spots develop. Clinical research shows a strong connection between this condition and sensitive skin. In a [clinical study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/) patients, an atopic background was detected in 85.5% of cases ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). The condition also frequently coexists with dry skin and atopic dermatitis ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)).

When a child has sensitive or atopic skin, the surface barrier loses moisture easily. This loss leads to microscopic irritation in the outer layer of the skin. As a result, the transfer of natural pigment slows down temporarily, leaving a lighter patch.

Weather changes also trigger these marks. Pityriasis alba lesions are documented to be more common and more apparent in cold weather, when the skin is dry ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). In that study, 39% of patients experienced an exacerbation at the start of winter ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). In Pune, cooler and drier air during winter months can rapidly dry out young skin.

Sunlight also plays an important part in how visible these spots look. Hypopigmentation in pityriasis alba is more prominent in dark skin compared to white skin ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). The condition often presents after sun exposure because tanning of the surrounding skin makes the affected areas more prominent ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). ## Common Myths: Diet Deficiencies and Fungal Infections

When light spots appear on a child's face, family members or friends often suspect nutritional issues. People frequently assume that a lack of calcium, vitamins, or intestinal worms causes the pale colour. These assumptions can cause parents undue stress.

Clinical evidence shows that poor nutrition is rarely the cause. In a clinical study, only 2% of pityriasis alba patients showed a low growth profile and signs of nutritional deficiencies ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). While anaemia was found in 16.5% of patients and intestinal parasites in 15.5%, widespread nutritional deficiency was not a direct cause ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). Most affected children are healthy and well nourished.

Similarly, laboratory tests clear up fears about infection. Bacterial and fungal culture studies failed to reveal any bacterial or fungal infectious cause ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). This means the condition is not a contagious infection passed from child to child.

Because infections and poor diet are not the root cause, home remedies and unverified medicines do not help. Harsh soaps or random parasite treatments will not fix the issue. Instead, daily skin hydration and gentle care address the true underlying dryness.

## Clinical Checks: How Dermatologists Tell Conditions Apart

The main worry for many parents is whether a light mark could be vitiligo or a fungal infection. A clinical evaluation by a medical doctor helps tell these conditions apart.

### 1\. Pityriasis Alba vs. Vitiligo

Vitiligo causes total pigment loss, leaving chalk-white patches with sharp borders and no surface scale. In contrast, pityriasis alba presents as superficial hypopigmented macules with slight fine overlying scaling ([Clinico epidemiological study of pityriasis alba](https://ijdvl.com/clinico-epidemiological-study-of-pityriasis-alba/)). Under a Wood lamp examination, the hypopigmentation of pityriasis alba does not enhance, and there is no fluorescence ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)).

### 2\. Pityriasis Alba vs. Fungal Infections

Superficial fungal infections often cause discoloured patches on the chest or back. However, scrapings for mycology show that microscopy and fungal culture are negative in pityriasis alba ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). This test confirms that no active fungal infection is present.

### 3\. Post-Inflammatory Light Marks

In pityriasis alba, a patch often evolves into a post-inflammatory light macule without scale before it resolves ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). A doctor can evaluate whether a light mark is part of this natural sequence or stems from earlier skin irritation.

## Restoring the Skin: Daily Creams and Sun Protection

Because pityriasis alba links closely with dry skin, treatment focuses on restoring hydration. No treatment is necessary for asymptomatic pityriasis alba ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). The marks often clear up on their own as skin tone evens out.

Parents can adopt simple daily habits to keep their child's skin comfortable:

- Gentle cleansing: Wash the face with lukewarm water and mild cleansers. Avoid strong, drying soaps that strip away skin oils.
- Moisturise regularly: A moisturising cream may improve the dry appearance of the skin ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). Applying cream consistently keeps the surface smooth.
- Sun protection: Sunscreen use to minimise sun tanning can reduce the development or prominence of pityriasis alba ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). Shielding the skin keeps the contrast between pale spots and surrounding skin minimal.
- Avoid rubbing: Remind children not to rub their cheeks dry with rough towels. Patting the skin gently protects the delicate surface.

If redness and itch are present, a mild prescription anti-inflammatory cream can help reduce these symptoms ([Pityriasis alba (dry white patches)](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pityriasis-alba)). Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you. Parents should never use over-the-counter medicated creams on young facial skin without medical guidance.

## Frequently asked questions

### What causes pityriasis alba in children?

Pityriasis alba is a low-grade dermatitis linked to dry, sensitive skin and atopic dermatitis. It often becomes noticeable during cold weather or after sun exposure tans the surrounding skin.

### Is pityriasis alba caused by poor nutrition or worms?

Clinical studies show widespread nutritional deficiencies are not a direct cause, and bacterial or fungal cultures are negative. Most affected children are well-nourished and healthy.

### How is pityriasis alba treated?

Asymptomatic cases do not require active medical treatment and resolve over time. Daily moisturising creams improve dryness, while sun protection reduces the contrast between pale patches and tanned skin.

### How does a doctor distinguish pityriasis alba from vitiligo?

Pityriasis alba presents with slight surface scaling and faint edges that do not enhance under a Wood lamp examination, unlike the chalk-white, non-scaling patches seen in vitiligo.
