## Managing Shaving Bumps: Understanding Pseudofolliculitis Barbae

Frequent shaving often causes a painful condition called [pseudofolliculitis barbae](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae), commonly known as razor bumps. Changing blades or shaving creams rarely solves the issue.

When you shave hair close to the skin, it leaves a sharp, angled tip. Razor bumps happen when this sharp tip pierces the skin surface or pierces the follicle wall from within ([Pseudofolliculitis Barbae (Razor Bumps): Images and Management](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)). The body treats the trapped hair shaft as an outside object, sparking an inflammatory reaction ([Pseudofolliculitis Barbae (Razor Bumps): Images and Management](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)).

This condition is more common in people with coarse, curly hair and darker skin tones ([Pseudofolliculitis Barbae (Razor Bumps): Images and Management](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)). When curled hair grows back, it bends toward the skin. This triggers fresh rounds of irritation after each shave. Over time, healed bumps can lead to complications such as dark marks (postinflammatory hyperpigmentation) and scarring, including keloids ([Pseudofolliculitis Barbae (Razor Bumps): Images and Management](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)).

Laser epilation using an Nd:YAG laser is an effective treatment option for pseudofolliculitis barbae ([Pseudofolliculitis Barbae (Razor Bumps): Images and Management](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)). It delivers light energy directly into the follicle. This helps calm active inflammation and lowers the chance of long-term scarring and dark marks ([Pseudofolliculitis Barbae (Razor Bumps): Images and Management](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)).

## Customising Care for Coarse Hair and Indian Skin Tones

Male facial hair needs careful attention. Beard follicles are thick, deep, and sensitive to male hormones. Settings used for fine body hair are not suitable for a man's neck or jawline.

A dermatologist examines key details before setting laser parameters. Treatment success depends on the hair's location, colour, coarseness, and stage in the growth cycle ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)).

- Heat Absorption: Coarse beard hair holds high amounts of melanin and absorbs light energy quickly. Laser pulses must be controlled carefully to prevent surface heat damage or follicle irritation.
- Protecting Indian Skin: Indian skin has melanin in both the hair follicle and the surrounding skin. Custom laser settings ensure energy bypasses surface pigment safely to reach the deep root.
- Smooth Results: Inconsistent coverage or fixed machine settings can cause patchy results. Proper adjustments create an even, clean border.
- Growth Cycle Stage: Hair follicles move through growing and resting phases. Treatment success depends on where the hair is in its growth cycle when treated ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)). A dermatologist considers this cycle when mapping out care ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)).

## Pre-Treatment Guidelines: How to Prepare for Your Visit

Good preparation before a clinical session ensures safe care and predictable results. Following medical advice protects the skin barrier and helps the laser work as intended.

- Sun Protection: Avoid indoor and outdoor tanning, as well as sunless tanning lotions ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)). Tanned skin holds extra pigment near the surface, raising the risk of skin burns. Apply a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with an SPF of 30 or higher every day before stepping outdoors ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)).
- Medicine Disclosures: Tell your dermatologist about all prescription and non-prescription medicines you take, including isotretinoin or aspirin ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)). Some medicines make the skin more sensitive to light.
- History of Scars and Outbreaks: You must tell your dermatologist if you have a tendency to scar, have ever had a keloid, or get outbreaks of cold sores or genital herpes ([Laser hair removal: Preparation](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-hair-removal-preparation)).
- Shaving Instructions: Do not pluck, thread, or wax before your session. Follow the specific trimming or shaving guidelines given by your clinic so the follicle root remains in place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does laser hair removal mean losing all facial hair?

No. In male grooming, laser treatments are often used to define beard borders along the upper cheeks and neck or to thin dense patches, leaving the central beard intact.

### How does laser treatment help with shaving bumps?

Pseudofolliculitis barbae occurs when sharp, shaved hair tips pierce the skin or follicle wall. Laser epilation targets the follicle directly, helping calm active inflammation and lowering the chance of dark marks and scarring.

### What should I do to prepare before a laser session?

Avoid tanning, apply daily broad-spectrum water-resistant sunscreen with an SPF of 30 or higher, and avoid plucking or waxing. You must also disclose all medications and any history of scars or keloids to your dermatologist.
