## Recognising Paediatric Impetigo in Pune: Honey-Coloured Crusts and Fragile Blisters

Impetigo is most prevalent in children between the ages of two and five years, though it can occur at any stage of childhood ([Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html), [Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)). The infection often develops in areas where there has been an injury or minor break in the skin barrier, such as a cut, an insect bite, or other trauma ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)). Clinically, impetigo presents in two primary forms: non-bullous and bullous ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)).

### Non-Bullous Impetigo

Non-bullous impetigo accounts for approximately 70 percent of all clinical cases and can be caused by Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pyogenes, or a combination of both ([Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)). It typically progresses through a recognisable series of visual stages ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html)):

- It begins as small, flat macules ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores)) or papules ([Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html)) that progress into fluid-filled pustules or vesicles ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores)).
- These vesicles burst or leak serous fluid onto the skin ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores)).
- As the fluid dries, it solidifies into characteristic golden, [honey-coloured crusts](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html) or scabs ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)).
- These lesions are most commonly seen on the face and extremities such as the arms and legs ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)).

### Bullous Impetigo

Bullous impetigo is responsible for roughly 30 percent of cases and presents differently on the skin ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)). Bullous impetigo manifests as superficial, thin-roofed, fluid-filled blisters that burst and leak serous fluid ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)). Once ruptured, they leak fluid and may leave scaled rims rather than the thick golden crusts seen in the non-bullous variant ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores)). Bullous impetigo is caused by toxin-producing strains of Staphylococcus aureus ([Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)).

## How School Sores Spread in Classrooms and Play Areas

In Pune classrooms, day-cares, and nurseries, impetigo spreads easily among young children. Bacterial transmission can occur through direct physical contact with an infected individual ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)) or through sharing contaminated household items such as clothing, towels, and bed linens ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)).

To prevent transmission across classroom settings and playgroups, clear isolation protocols should be observed:

- School and nursery exclusion: Children with suspected impetigo should remain home from school or child-care services until appropriate antibiotic treatment has been started ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)).
- Infectious window: With appropriate medical treatment, impetigo is usually no longer contagious within 24 to 48 hours ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment), [Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)). Guidance indicates individuals may return to school at least 12 hours after initiating antibiotic treatment, provided their lesions remain covered ([Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html)).
- Covering lesions: Any sores located on exposed skin, such as the face, arms, or legs, must be securely covered with a waterproof dressing when returning to a group environment ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html)).
- Avoiding close contact activities: Because children are more likely to spread the infection through close physical contact such as during playtime or contact sports, these activities should be paused while contagious ([Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)).

## Clinical Dermatologist Evaluation and Medical Treatment Classes

A frequent mistake parents make when faced with weeping sores is applying leftover household creams, adult acne washes, or unverified soothing ointments. Applying a prescription anti-inflammatory medicine intended for eczema or adult skin rashes without a doctor's advice can suppress local immunity and cause bacterial infections to spread more aggressively across delicate tissue. Consulting an MD dermatologist ensures that your child's skin is properly evaluated and treated with the correct class of medication.

Depending on the extent, location, and presentation of the lesions, an MD dermatologist can recommend a targeted therapeutic approach:

- Prescription antibiotic ointment: For limited, localised non-bullous lesions, a dermatologist may suggest a targeted topical antibiotic ointment applied directly to the skin ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)). Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you.
- Prescription oral antibiotic: When impetigo involves multiple lesions, bullous blisters, or extensive infection, a doctor may recommend a prescription oral antibiotic targeting the underlying bacteria ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores), [Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)). Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you.
- Medicated cleansers: In managing transmission or cleansing infected areas, a doctor may advise washing with an antibacterial soap or cleanser to help reduce surface bacteria ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)). Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you.
- Nasal assessment: In recurrent outbreaks where impetigo returns frequently, a dermatologist may evaluate whether bacteria are colonising inside the nostrils and consider topical applications to clear them ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment)). Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you.

Completing the full course of therapy advised by the doctor is an essential part of clinical care, as stopping early may allow surviving bacteria to multiply and lead to a swift recurrence.

## Gentle Wound Hygiene and Household Containment Measures

Administering medical treatment goes hand in hand with consistent, gentle wound care at home. [Soaking the affected skin in warm water](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment) and soap helps to gently remove dirt and crusts ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment)), which may also aid in symptomatic relief while medical therapies take effect ([Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)).

Helpful home nursing protocols include:

- Softening crusts gently: Wash the skin lesions with soap and water every 8 to 12 hours, gently soaking to soften and remove visible crusts ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores)), and avoid scrubbing the delicate skin during washing ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)).
- Soaking carefully: Soaking the affected skin in warm water and soap helps to gently remove dirt and crusts prior to applying prescribed medicine ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment)).
- Patting dry: After each wash, pat the skin lesions dry using a clean towel ([Impetigo (school sores) | health.vic.gov.au](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/impetigo-school-sores)).
- Keeping fingernails short: Keeping your child's nails short and filed smooth prevents further skin damage and discourages them from scratching or picking at their skin ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)).
- Dedicated laundry: Clothing, bed linens, and towels used by someone with impetigo should be washed every day and not shared with others in the household ([Clinical Guidance for Group A Streptococcal Impetigo | Group A Stre…](https://www.cdc.gov/group-a-strep/hcp/clinical-guidance/impetigo.html)); washing these items in hot water helps prevent transmission ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo)).
- Frequent hand hygiene: Encourage your child and family members to wash their hands frequently with soap and water, especially after touching lesions or dressings ([Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo), [Impetigo: Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2014/0815/p229)).
- Supervised dilute baths: For children experiencing frequent impetigo outbreaks, a dermatologist may recommend adding small amounts of regular bleach to bathwater to help lower skin bacteria ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment), [Impetigo | Treatment & Helpful Tips](https://www.cincinnatichildrens.org/health/i/impetigo), [Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)). Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you.

## Protecting Delicate Skin: Preventing Scars and Long-Term Marks

A primary worry for parents is whether these raw, crusted sores will leave lasting marks on their child's face. Impetigo lesions typically heal without leaving scars unless scratching cuts deep into the skin ([Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)).

However, scarring can become a real risk if complications occur ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment), [Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)). When lesions are aggressively scratched, repeatedly picked at, or left untreated, the bacterial infection can penetrate deeper into the skin ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment), [Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)). This deeper ulcerative complication is known as [ecthyma](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo) ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment), [Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)). Ecthyma produces ulcerative sores that penetrate deeper into the skin layers, which can lead to scars forming as the skin heals ([Impetigo: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/impetigo-treatment), [Treating Pediatric and Adolescent Impetigo](https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/treating-pediatric-and-adolescent-impetigo)).

In addition, superficial inflammation on young skin can leave behind temporary discolouration as healing progresses. Keeping the skin clean, avoiding rough friction, and preventing your child from scratching or peeling scabs will protect delicate tissue and support healthy recovery.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does paediatric impetigo look like?

Non-bullous impetigo begins as small spots or fluid-filled vesicles that burst and dry into characteristic honey-coloured crusts. The bullous type forms thin-roofed blisters that burst, leak fluid, and leave raw rims on the skin.

### When can a child return to school after having impetigo?

Children should remain home until medical treatment has begun. With appropriate antibiotic treatment, lesions are typically no longer contagious within 24 to 48 hours, and children may return at least 12 hours after starting treatment provided lesions stay covered with a waterproof dressing.

### Does impetigo leave long-lasting scars on children?

Impetigo sores generally heal without long-lasting scarring unless they are scratched deeply. Repeated picking or scratching can cause the infection to progress deeper into ulcerative sores called ecthyma, which can leave scars.

### How can families prevent impetigo from spreading at home?

Prevent spread by washing hands frequently with soap and water, keeping the child's fingernails trimmed short, washing their clothes, towels, and bed linens daily in hot water without sharing, and keeping sores covered.
