## Clinical Diagnosis for Freckles Treatment: Ephelides, Lentigines, and Melasma

Before starting any procedure, a dermatologist must assess the type and depth of pigment in the skin. Spots that look similar to the naked eye often have very different biological causes.

- Ephelides (Freckles): Small, flat, light brown or reddish spots that often link to genetics. They darken quickly during bright summer months and fade partially when sun exposure drops.
- Solar Lentigines (Sun or Age Spots): Distinct, flat brown spots caused by cumulative sun exposure over time. These spots represent localized pigment deposits in the upper skin that persist throughout the entire year.
- Melasma: Symmetrical, irregular brownish patches that appear mainly across the cheeks, nose, forehead, and upper lip. Melasma involves deeper hormonal triggers, vascular activity, and pigment in both upper and deeper skin layers.

Knowing the exact difference between these conditions is essential. Freckles and solar lentigines sit mainly in the upper epidermal layer. In contrast, melasma often reaches deeper into the dermis. When people apply harsh scrubs or bleaching creams to melasma, pigment-producing cells can react defensively. This irritation often results in [deeper discoloration](/blog/pigmentation-around-mouth-treatment-in-pune) that becomes harder to correct.

During an in-person consultation, a dermatologist examines the skin using medical magnification and focused lighting. Checking the border, shade, and depth of each spot allows the doctor to design a custom treatment plan suited to your specific phototype.

## How Chemical Peels Accelerate Epidermal Melanin Clearance

Chemical peels offer an effective clinical way to treat surface pigment. A dermatologist applies a controlled chemical solution to loosen dead surface cells. This gentle exfoliation helps shed excess melanin and reveals fresher, more uniform skin underneath.

Among available peeling therapies, glycolic acid peels are utilized to lighten hyperpigmented lesions in epidermal hypermelanosis ([Postinflammatory hyperpigmentation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/postinflammatory-hyperpigmentation)). Glycolic acid works by gently loosening darkened surface cells in the outer epidermis. This measured process gradually lifts surface spots while smoothing rough skin texture.

However, physical treatments require careful handling in Indian skin tones. Chemical peels can help epidermal pigmentation, but physical treatments can also aggravate discoloration by injuring the epidermis ([Postinflammatory hyperpigmentation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/postinflammatory-hyperpigmentation)). In darker complexions, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation tends to be more intense and persist longer ([Postinflammatory hyperpigmentation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/postinflammatory-hyperpigmentation)). For this reason, dermatologists select mild peeling agents and watch the skin closely throughout the visit.

Dermatologists manage peel concentration, skin contact duration, and rapid neutralization to keep the skin barrier healthy. Peels can also be scheduled to address [residual post-acne marks](/blog/post-acne-pigmentation-treatment-kalyani-nagar) alongside sun spots. This dual focus improves overall skin clarity without subjecting the skin to excessive stress.

## Laser Therapy and Q-Switched Modalities for Melanin-Rich Skin

When stubborn freckles or sun spots do not clear with topical routines and mild peels, laser treatments offer a helpful second step. However, using lasers on darker skin tones requires careful calibration to prevent side effects.

In skin of colour, melanin can act as a [competitive chromophore](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour), which is a coloured molecule that absorbs transmitted energy ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). This competitive absorption increases the risk of side effects after epidermal injury by a laser ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). Because of variable results, treatment costs, and complication risks, lasers remain [second line to topical agents](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36781686/) for post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in skin of colour ([Review of Laser Treatments for Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36781686/)).

For cases resistant to topicals, laser devices such as neodymium:yttrium-aluminum-garnet (Nd:YAG) and fractional photothermolysis systems can provide adjunctive treatment in skin of colour when clinicians use appropriate parameters ([Review of Laser Treatments for Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36781686/)). In clinical research evaluating Asian patients with facial lentigines, both 660-nm and 532-nm [Q-switched Nd:YAG lasers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26551773/) effectively reduced pigmentation for up to 8 weeks with high patient satisfaction ([Q-Switched 660-nm Versus 532-nm Nd: YAG Laser for the Treatment for…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26551773/)). In a study of Korean women with Fitzpatrick skin types III and IV, researchers noted no significant differences in severity index scores between the two lasers ([Q-Switched 660-nm Versus 532-nm Nd: YAG Laser for the Treatment for…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26551773/)). However, the average melanin level was significantly reduced by the 660-nm laser at week 8 compared to baseline, whereas the 532-nm laser did not show that significant decrease ([Q-Switched 660-nm Versus 532-nm Nd: YAG Laser for the Treatment for…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26551773/)).

To protect against epidermal thermal injury during laser therapy in skin of colour, clinicians incorporate specific safeguards ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)):

- Epidermal Cooling: Contact cooling protects the epidermis from thermal burns, using active tools like sapphire laser windows or copper tips, or passive methods like ice cubes ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). Non-contact cooling uses cold air convection or cryo-cooling ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)).
- Test Pulses: A test pulse or pulses can be used to check the immediate effects of the laser on the skin ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)).
- Long Wavelengths and Pulse Times: An Nd:YAG laser with a long wavelength and wider pulse times is often the most suitable device for use in skin of colour to reduce epidermal damage ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)).
- Multiple Sessions: Dividing care into multiple, short sessions helps reduce epidermal damage ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)).

## Aligning Pigmentation Care with Acne and Laser Hair Reduction Plans

Many people dealing with sun spots also manage other common concerns, such as active acne breakouts, acne scars, or unwanted facial hair. Coordinating these treatments in the right clinical order protects the skin barrier and prevents complications.

When active acne breakouts are present, they should be medically controlled first before starting procedures for surface dark spots. Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation affects phototypes rich in melanin most frequently and severely, and it often follows a chronic, unpredictable course ([Review of Laser Treatments for Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36781686/)). In addition, post-inflammatory pigmentation is more common, darker, and longer-lasting in darker skin types ([Postinflammatory hyperpigmentation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/postinflammatory-hyperpigmentation)). Once active spots heal, dermatologists can plan safe procedures for acne scars alongside residual sun spots.

Device selection is also important when planning facial hair reduction alongside pigmentation care. The long-pulsed 1064 nm Nd:YAG laser is considered a suitable laser for hair removal in individuals with skin of colour ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). Its 1064 nm wavelength sits at the end of the melanin absorption spectrum ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). This property allows the laser to spare the epidermis while causing sufficient thermal injury to dark, coarse hair ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)).

Other hair removal lasers require caution in deeper complexions. Alexandrite lasers (755 nm) have been reported to cause blistering in patients with Fitzpatrick skin types V and VI ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). In comparison, diode lasers (800 nm) have been reported to be mainly safe ([Laser therapy in skin of colour](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/laser-therapy-in-skin-of-colour)). A dermatologist will schedule sessions at proper intervals so hair removal and pigment treatments support each other safely.

## Essential Photoprotection and Aftercare to Prevent Recurrence

Clinical treatments can lighten existing sun spots, but they cannot prevent future melanin production if sun exposure continues. Without careful daily sun protection, treated spots can darken again.

Daily application of an SPF 50+ broad-spectrum sunscreen on exposed areas is important to minimize darkening caused by ultraviolet radiation ([Postinflammatory hyperpigmentation](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/postinflammatory-hyperpigmentation)). Sunscreen should be applied every morning across the face and neck. It is needed on cloudy days and indoors near open windows, as ultraviolet rays easily penetrate standard window glass.

After a chemical peel or laser session, the skin needs [gentle care to heal well](/blog/aftercare-after-cosmetic-procedures-pune). Patients should wash with mild, non-drying cleansers and apply soothing moisturizers to support the skin barrier. Abrasive scrubs, rough sponges, and strong active exfoliants should be avoided until the skin fully recovers. If mild irritation develops, your doctor might recommend a short course of a prescription anti-inflammatory cream. Your dermatologist will advise whether this is suitable for you.

Periodic follow-up visits allow the dermatologist to check skin recovery, adjust daily skin care routines, and adapt sun protection habits as seasonal weather changes in Pune.

## Frequently asked questions

### How are freckles different from melasma on Indian skin?

Freckles and solar lentigines are superficial pigment spots located primarily in the outer epidermal layer of the skin. Melasma is a deeper condition triggered by hormonal and vascular factors that often extends into the dermis.

### Can chemical peels safely remove freckles on darker skin tones?

Glycolic acid chemical peels can help lighten epidermal pigmentation by shedding excess melanin from darkened surface cells. However, they must be chosen and monitored carefully by a dermatologist to prevent post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.

### Which laser is suitable for treating pigmentation in skin of colour?

Nd:YAG lasers, including Q-switched modalities, provide adjunctive treatment for resistant pigment when used with appropriate parameters. Safeguards like epidermal cooling and test pulses are used to reduce the risk of thermal injury.

### What aftercare is needed after clinical pigmentation treatments?

Patients should use broad-spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen daily, wash with mild cleansers, and avoid abrasive scrubs or strong exfoliants while the skin heals. A dermatologist may recommend a soothing moisturizer or a prescription anti-inflammatory cream if irritation occurs.

This information is general and not a substitute for a consultation. Results vary from person to person.
